Course Introduction
Brand Identity Design teaches learners how to turn brand strategy into a coherent visual and verbal system.
The course moves from discovery and positioning through logo systems, colour, typography, imagery, voice,
guidelines, applications, sub-brand consistency, audits and professional handoff.


Learning Objectives
   Conduct structured brand discovery and audience research.
   Develop positioning, personality and core messaging.
   Create an original flexible logo system.
   Build functional colour and typography systems.
   Define supporting graphics and image direction.
   Create brand voice and layout rules.
   Produce useful brand guidelines.
   Apply identity across digital and print touchpoints.
   Complete and hand off an organised mini brand identity system.

Lesson 1: Introduction to Brand Identity
Design
Introduction
Brand identity is the planned system of visual and verbal elements that helps people recognise, understand
and remember an organisation. A logo is important, but it is only one part of the identity.


Learning Objectives
   Explain the core principles of introduction to brand identity design.
   Complete a guided brand-design practical for introduction to brand identity design.
   Apply the lesson to an Ethan Digital Academy identity scenario.


Teaching Content
Brand principle: Brand identity includes logo, colour, typography, imagery, layout style and supporting
graphic elements.

Brand principle: Brand strategy explains the meaning and direction behind the visible identity.

Brand principle: Consistency strengthens recognition, but consistency does not mean every design must
look identical.

Brand principle: A useful identity must work in real contexts and at different sizes.

Brand principle: Originality and responsible use of intellectual property are essential.

Lesson 4: Naming, Taglines and Brand
Architecture
Introduction
Names and taglines help audiences understand and remember brands, while brand architecture explains
relationships between a main brand and its products or sub-brands.


Learning Objectives
   Explain the core principles of naming, taglines and brand architecture.
   Complete a guided brand-design practical for naming, taglines and brand architecture.
   Apply the lesson to an Ethan Digital Academy identity scenario.


Teaching Content
Brand principle: A name should be distinctive, pronounceable and suitable for its context.

Brand principle: A tagline should add meaning rather than repeat the name.

Brand principle: Brand architecture can use a master brand, endorsed brands or more independent sub-
brands.

Brand principle: Naming decisions should consider domain, trademark and cultural checks before
commercial adoption.

Brand principle: A visual relationship between sub-brands should be deliberate.


Step-by-Step Guided Practical
1. Create three fictional brand names for a training service.

2. Write one tagline for each.

3. Choose the strongest and explain why.

4. Draw a simple map showing a master brand and three services.

5. Label which elements should stay visually related.

Lesson 6: Building a Logo System
Introduction
Professional identities often require several approved logo configurations for different spaces.


Learning Objectives
   Explain the core principles of building a logo system.
   Complete a guided brand-design practical for building a logo system.
   Apply the lesson to an Ethan Digital Academy identity scenario.


Teaching Content
Brand principle: A primary logo is the preferred full version.

Brand principle: A secondary lockup can fit different proportions.

Brand principle: An icon or symbol may serve compact spaces.

Brand principle: Monochrome versions support restricted colour situations.

Brand principle: Clear-space and minimum-size rules protect legibility.


Step-by-Step Guided Practical
1. Create a primary logo for a fictional brand.

2. Create one alternate arrangement.

3. Create a compact icon if the concept supports it.

4. Create one-colour versions.

5. Define a simple clear-space rule.

Professional Practice Checklist
   Begin with discovery and strategy before visual execution.
   Create original identity concepts rather than imitating existing brands.
   Test logos at small size and in one colour.
   Assign clear roles to colours and typography.
   Use authorised images and accurate claims.
   Document logo, colour, type, imagery and layout rules.
   Review identity across real touchpoints.
   Keep editable masters and approved exports organised.
   Confirm trademark, domain and licensing requirements before real commercial adoption.
