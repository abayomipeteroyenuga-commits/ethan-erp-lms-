COURSE OVERVIEW
Digital marketing is the strategic use of digital channels, content, technology and data to attract, engage,
convert and retain customers. This course teaches the full marketing process rather than isolated social-
media posting.

Learners will plan audiences, content, search visibility, email communication, social campaigns, paid-media
concepts, landing pages, lead generation, analytics and integrated campaign strategy using Ethan Digital
Academy as the main practical case.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
 Explain digital marketing and its major channels.
 Research audiences and create customer personas.
 Map the customer journey and marketing funnel.
 Develop positioning, offers and clear calls to action.
 Plan content pillars and content calendars.
 Apply social-media marketing principles.
 Understand SEO and search intent.
 Plan permission-based email marketing.
 Understand paid campaign structure and budgeting principles.
 Design lead-generation and landing-page journeys.
 Measure reach, engagement, clicks, leads and conversions.
 Build an integrated digital marketing campaign.

 Social media

 Social insights

LESSON 4: BRAND POSITIONING, VALUE PROPOSITION AND OFFERS
Positioning explains the place a brand wants to occupy in the customer's mind. A value proposition explains
why the offer matters.

 Figure 11. Brand consistency and value communication.

Clarify the target audience, problem, category, benefit and reason to believe.

Value Proposition

Offer

Call to Action

LESSON 16: CAMPAIGN BUDGETING AND MEDIA PLANNING
A media plan allocates budget across channels, time periods and objectives.

 Figure 8. Campaign structure should connect budget to objective and measurement.

Budget Types
Campaigns may use daily or total/lifetime budgets depending on platform and plan.

Allocation
Budget should reflect objectives, audience size, historical performance and testing needs.

Testing
Reserve some budget for controlled creative or audience tests rather than changing many variables at once.

Forecasting
Forecasts are estimates, not guarantees. Use ranges and update assumptions with real performance data.

Governance
Set spending limits and approval processes before launch.

LESSON 18: CONVERSION OPTIMISATION AND A/B TESTING
CONCEPTS
Conversion optimisation improves the percentage of visitors who complete a desired action.

 Figure 10. Analytics should lead to practical decisions.

Conversion Barriers
Confusing pages, weak value, slow loading, unclear forms and poor message match can reduce conversions.

A/B Testing
Compare two controlled versions of one important element, such as a headline or CTA, while keeping other
factors as stable as practical.

Hypothesis
State what you expect to improve and why.

Evidence
Use enough relevant data before drawing conclusions; small samples can be misleading.

Ethics
Do not use deceptive interfaces, hidden charges or manipulative dark patterns.

Step-by-Step Practical
1. Review a fictional landing page.

2. Identify five possible conversion barriers.

3. Write one A/B test hypothesis.

 

4. Create Version A and Version B headlines.

5. Choose the conversion metric.

Review Questions
1. What is conversion optimisation?

2. What is an A/B test?

3. What is a hypothesis?

4. Why can small samples mislead?

Assignment
Create a conversion-improvement plan.

Reviews and Reputation
Encourage authentic feedback; never fabricate reviews.

Complaints

Retention

Advocacy
Satisfied customers may voluntarily recommend the brand; advocacy should be earned, not forced.
