Course Introduction
Internet and email skills are essential for modern education, work and business. This course develops
confident browsing, effective research, professional email communication, file handling, privacy awareness,
account security and responsible online behaviour.

Learning Objectives
 Explain how the internet, web browsers and websites work at an everyday-user level.
 Search for information efficiently and evaluate online sources.
 Download, upload and organise files responsibly.
 Write, send and manage professional email.
 Recognise common phishing and scam warning signs without interacting with suspicious systems.
 Protect accounts, privacy and digital reputation.
 Combine research, files, email and scheduling into a complete workflow.

Lesson 1: Internet Foundations and Everyday
Uses
Introduction
The internet is a global system of interconnected networks that allows devices and services to exchange
information. Learners use it for communication, education, research, banking, entertainment, cloud
applications and many other legitimate activities.

Learning Objectives
 Explain the main concepts in internet foundations and everyday uses.
 Complete a guided practical related to internet foundations and everyday uses.
 Apply safe, responsible and professional digital practices.

Teaching Content
Key point: The internet is the underlying network; the World Wide Web is one service that uses it.

Key point: A device usually connects through a local network and an internet service provider before
reaching an online service.

Key point: Online services depend on connectivity, servers, software and user accounts.

Key point: Good digital practice includes protecting accounts, respecting privacy and checking information
before relying on it.

 

 
 Figure 1: Instructional visual for Internet Foundations and Everyday Uses.

Step-by-Step Guided Practical
1. List five legitimate ways you use the internet.

2. Identify whether each example is communication, research, cloud productivity or entertainment.

3. Draw a simple device-to-online-service connection path.

4. Explain the difference between the internet and the web.

Ethan Digital Academy Case Study

instructors and use authorised cloud applications.

Lesson 2: Web Browsers, Tabs and Web

Introduction
A web browser is software used to access websites and web applications. Understanding the address bar,

Learning Objectives
 Explain the main concepts in web browsers, tabs and web addresses.
 Complete a guided practical related to web browsers, tabs and web addresses.
 Apply safe, responsible and professional digital practices.

Teaching Content
Key point: Common browser controls include Back, Forward, Refresh, Home where configured, tabs and
the address bar.

Key point: A URL is the address of a web resource. The domain name is an important part of that address.

Key point: HTTPS indicates encrypted transport between browser and site, but users must still evaluate

Lesson 3: Searching the Web Effectively
Introduction
Search engines help users locate information on the web. Better searches begin with a clear question and
specific keywords rather than long, vague sentences.

Learning Objectives
 Explain the main concepts in searching the web effectively.
 Complete a guided practical related to searching the web effectively.
 Apply safe, responsible and professional digital practices.

Teaching Content
Key point: Use important nouns, names, dates or phrases that describe the information needed.

Key point: Quotation marks may help search for an exact phrase in many search engines.

Lesson 4: Evaluating Online Information and

Lesson 6: Email Foundations and Email

Introduction
Email is a system for sending digital messages between addresses. A professional email address and

Learning Objectives
 Explain the main concepts in email foundations and email addresses.
 Complete a guided practical related to email foundations and email addresses.
 Apply safe, responsible and professional digital practices.

Teaching Content
Key point: An email address normally contains a local name, @ symbol and domain.

Key point: The To field contains primary recipients.

Key point: CC sends a visible copy to secondary recipients; BCC can protect recipient visibility in

Lesson 10: Phishing, Scams and Suspicious
Messages
Introduction
Phishing is an attempt to trick people into revealing sensitive information or taking an unsafe action.
Awareness is more important than trying to investigate suspicious systems yourself.

Learning Objectives
 Explain the main concepts in phishing, scams and suspicious messages.
 Complete a guided practical related to phishing, scams and suspicious messages.
 Apply safe, responsible and professional digital practices.

Teaching Content
Key point: Warning signs can include unusual urgency, unexpected payment requests, sender mismatches,
unfamiliar links and requests for passwords or security codes.

Key point: A familiar logo or display name does not prove a message is genuine.

Key point: When uncertain, contact the organisation through a known official channel rather than replying to
the suspicious message.

Key point: Do not forward suspicious links to friends for them to test.

Key point: Use the email provider's report-phishing or spam feature where appropriate.

 

 
 Figure 10: Instructional visual for Phishing, Scams and Suspicious Messages.

Step-by-Step Guided Practical
1. Review a fictional suspicious-message example supplied by the instructor.

2. Circle or list four warning signs.

3. Explain how you would verify the request using an official channel.

4. State what information you should never send by email.

5. Practise locating the Report spam/phishing control without reporting a legitimate message.

Ethan Digital Academy Case Study
If a message claims an Academy account will be closed unless a password is sent immediately, the
recipient should not comply and should verify through authorised Academy support.

Lesson 11: Passwords, MFA and Account
Recovery
Introduction
Email accounts often unlock many other online services, so protecting them is essential. Strong
authentication reduces the chance that one stolen password leads to wider account loss.

Learning Objectives
 Explain the main concepts in passwords, mfa and account recovery.
 Complete a guided practical related to passwords, mfa and account recovery.
 Apply safe, responsible and professional digital practices.

Teaching Content
Key point: Use a long, unique password or passphrase for important accounts.

Key point: Do not reuse the same password across unrelated services.

Key point: Multi-factor authentication adds another verification step.

Key point: Never share one-time codes with someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

Key point: Keep recovery email or phone information current where appropriate.

Step-by-Step Guided Practical
1. Without revealing your password, write the characteristics of a strong passphrase.

2. Check whether your email provider offers MFA.

3. Review recovery options on your own account if permitted.

4. Write a safe response to someone asking for your one-time code.

5. Explain why password reuse is risky.

Ethan Digital Academy Case Study

Course Completion Outcome
On successful completion, the learner should be able to browse and search the web confidently, evaluate
online information, manage downloads and uploads, communicate professionally by email, organise
messages, handle attachments responsibly, recognise common phishing warning signs, protect account
privacy and combine internet research with a reliable communication workflow.

Professional Practice Checklist
 Verify websites and recipients before sharing information.
 Use strong, unique authentication for important accounts.
 Do not share passwords or one-time codes.
 Pause and verify unexpected links, attachments and urgent requests.
 Use professional language in academic and workplace communication.
 Respect privacy, copyright and online conduct rules.
